The Mechanics Bench

The CIE Mechanics setups, alive: a block on a slope, five connected-particle rigs (slope, twin slopes, table, table with two hanging masses, Atwood), three collisions and a projectile. Every force arrow is drawn to scale in newtons, the acceleration is solved live, and in every rope rig the string length is designed so that when one particle lands the other keeps moving on its own momentum, slows, stops short of the pulley, and (where it can) slides back until the string snaps taut again. The clock prints each of those moments.

The misconception this kills

Physics: g = 9.8 m/s², μₛ = μₖ; statics checked before kinetics (nothing moves unless the driving force exceeds μN). Rope rigs: the taut phase, the slack phase (free particle decelerating under gravity and friction alone) and the return phase are all closed-form s = ut + ½at², so the printed times are exact; the string length is chosen per scenario so the free particle stops at least 0.40 m short of the pulley. Collisions: Newton's experimental law with restitution e; the slope collision is integrated at 1200 steps per second with static/kinetic friction. Force arrows share one scale in newtons.
