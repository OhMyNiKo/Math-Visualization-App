Wavefunction in 3D

A quantum particle in a two-dimensional box, drawn as the standing wave it actually is. Grab the surface and drag to spin the camera to any angle; raise the quantum numbers and watch nodal lines appear that never, ever move.

The solutions \(\displaystyle \psi_{n_x n_y}(x,y)=\frac{2}{L}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{n_x\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\!\left(\frac{n_y\pi y}{L}\right) \)  ·  \(\displaystyle E=(n_x^{2}+n_y^{2})\,\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2mL^{2}} \)

The misconception this kills

A wavefunction is not a fuzzy picture of "where the particle orbits". It is a standing wave. Spin the camera: the nodal lines you see from above are real places where the particle is simply never found, and while the surface breathes in time, those lines never move. Swap nₓ and nₔ and the energy stays exactly the same: that is degeneracy, made visible.
