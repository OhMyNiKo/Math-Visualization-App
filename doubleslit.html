Double-Slit Interference

One source, one barrier with two slits, one screen: every distance and the wavelength are yours to change. The field between barrier and screen is computed point by point from wave superposition, and the screen shows its intensity as colour.

The defining equation \(\displaystyle I(y)\propto\cos^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi d\,y}{\lambda D}\right)\,\operatorname{sinc}^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi a\,y}{\lambda D}\right) \)  · bright fringes \(\displaystyle \Delta y=\lambda D/d \)

The misconception this kills

Waves do not add like intensities. Amplitudes add first, then you square: two bright waves can make a dark stripe, and the first dark fringe here really reads ? of the central maximum. Widen λ and the stripes spread; the spacing is λD/d, set by the wave itself, not by the slits "being close together".

Physics: Huygens superposition, each slit sampled as 7 secondary sources; complex field A = Σ eik(r₀+r)/√r summed at every screen and field point, intensity |A|² (k = 2π/λ). The Fraunhofer formula above is the far-field limit of exactly this sum. Check: spacing measured from the computed screen agrees with λD₂/d to a few percent.
