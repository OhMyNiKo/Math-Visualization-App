The Normal Distribution and Standardisation

Slide the mean and the standard deviation and watch the bell on the left slide and stretch while the standard bell on the right never moves. Drag the two bounds \(x_1 < x_2\) on either plot: the shaded probability is computed under both curves and is always the same number. Press Standardise to see \(X\) squashed onto \(Z\).

The defining equation \(\displaystyle Z = \frac{X-\mu}{\sigma},\quad f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-\frac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}} \) · \(\displaystyle P(x_1 < X < x_2) = \Phi(z_2)-\Phi(z_1) \)

Controls

Amber marks: μ±σ holds 68.3%, μ±2σ 95.4%, μ±3σ 99.7%. Both plots share one vertical scale, so heights are comparable.

The misconception this kills

"A larger σ just makes the bell wider." It cannot: the area under any density is exactly 1, so when σ grows the peak \( \tfrac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} \) must fall, and when σ shrinks it must rise. And the standard table is not a different distribution: standardising only relabels the axis, so the area under \(Z\) between \(z_1\) and \(z_2\) is the very same probability as under \(X\): right now ?.

Mathematics: \( \Phi(z)=\tfrac12\,\mathrm{erfc}(-z/\sqrt2) \), with erfc from the exponentially weighted power series \( \mathrm{erf}(x)=\tfrac{2}{\sqrt\pi}e^{-x^2}\sum 2^n x^{2n+1}/(2n+1)!! \) for \(|x|<2\) and the Laplace continued fraction (100 convergents) beyond: relative error below \(10^{-13}\) in both tails, absolute error below \(10^{-15}\), better than any printed table. The morph interpolates \((\mu,\sigma)\to(0,1)\) while holding the bounds at fixed \(z\), so the shaded area is preserved exactly.
