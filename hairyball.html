The Hairy Ball Theorem

A sphere covered in hair, and a comb: you. Switch to comb mode and try, with all the patience you have, to flatten every hair. The red bald spots will move wherever you push them — and they will never, ever disappear.

The misconception this kills

It is not lack of combing skill. On a sphere, every continuous field of tangent hair has a zero — the indices of the bald spots must total χ = 2, so you can merge the two cowlicks into one double cowlick, or split them, but never reach zero of them. A torus (χ = 0) combs flat; a sphere never does. Same theorem, other outfit: at every moment there is at least one point on Earth with no horizontal wind at all.

Mathematics: 1,000 hairs on a latitude–longitude grid, each a tangent vector; combing projects your stroke direction onto the local tangent plane and blends it in with a smooth brush. Bald spots are hairs shorter than 18 percent of full length, clustered. The Poincaré–Hopf theorem forces Σ index = χ(S²) = 2 for any continuous choice, which is why the counter never reads zero.
