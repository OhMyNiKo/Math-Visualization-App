Complex Arithmetic as Geometry

Drag the two complex numbers \(z\) (teal) and \(w\) (blue) around the Argand diagram and pick an operation. Adding is a translation; multiplying is a turn by \(\arg w\) followed by a stretch by \(|w|\). Replay animates the turn-then-stretch so you can see the product being built.

Polar forms, live \(\displaystyle z = r e^{i\theta} \)

Controls

Arcs at the origin are the arguments θz = arg z, θw = arg w; the faint circle is |z| = 1. Result in amber.

The misconception this kills

"Multiplying makes numbers bigger." Not in \(\mathbb{C}\): multiplying by \(w\) stretches by \(|w|\) and turns by \(\arg w\), and if \(|w| = 1\) (like \(i\)) nothing gets bigger at all, the number only rotates. Press "multiply by i" and watch \(z\) turn a quarter-circle with its length untouched. Right now \(w\) turns \(z\) by ?.

Mathematics: exact Cartesian arithmetic for \(+,\ -,\ \times,\ \div\); modulus by hypot, argument by atan2 in \((-\pi,\pi]\); the animated product draws \( z\,e^{i s\arg w} \) for \(s\) from 0 to 1, then scales the radius from \(|z|\) to \(|z||w|\) (division uses \(-\arg w\) and \(1/|w|\)).
