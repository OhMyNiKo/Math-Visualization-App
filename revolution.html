Volumes of Revolution (Disc Method)

Spin the graph of \( f \) about the x-axis and the region under it sweeps out a solid. Slice that solid into \( n \) thin discs of radius \( f(x_k) \): their volumes add up to a Riemann sum that closes in on \( \pi\!\int_a^b f^2\,dx \). Choose the curve, drag the bounds and the disc count, spin the profile with the rotation slider, and replay to watch the discs stack in one by one.

The defining equation \(\displaystyle V = \pi \int_a^b [f(x)]^2\,dx \) · disc sum \(\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{n} \pi\,[f(x_k)]^2\,\Delta x \) · washer with a hole of radius c: \(\displaystyle V=\pi\int_a^b \big|[f(x)]^2-c^2\big|\,dx \)

Controls

Supported: + − * / ^ ( ), implicit products, sin cos tan atan exp ln sqrt sinh cosh tanh, pi and e. The radius used is |f(x)|.

The misconception this kills

"Twice the height, twice the volume." No: a disc's volume goes with the square of its radius, \( \pi r^2\,\Delta x \), so doubling \( f \) everywhere multiplies every disc, and the whole solid, by four, not two. Recomputing the integral right now with \( 2f \) in place of \( f \) multiplies V by ?.

Mathematics: the disc sum uses midpoints \( x_k=a+(k-\tfrac12)\Delta x \); the exact volume is \( \pi\int_a^b |f^2-c^2|\,dx \) by composite Simpson with 2000 panels (error \( O(h^4) \) on smooth stretches); the picture is an oblique orthographic projection with the axis of revolution horizontal and each disc drawn as an ellipse.
