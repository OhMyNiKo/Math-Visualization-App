Spacetime Diagram

The lab's grid of space and time, and on top of it, in teal, the grid of an observer flying past at speed v. Slide v and watch their axes scissor toward the light cone; drag the two events and see "at the same time" fall apart.

The defining equation \(\displaystyle x'=\gamma(x-vt) \)  ·  \(\displaystyle t'=\gamma\left(t-\frac{vx}{c^{2}}\right) \)  ·  \(\displaystyle \gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}/c^{2}}} \)  (units c=1)

Controls

Drag events A and B anywhere on the diagram. The dashed teal line through A is the moving observer's "now": everything on it happens, for them, at the same moment.

The misconception this kills

"Now" is not universal. In the lab frame A and B are separated in time by ?; for the moving observer, by ? — slide v and you can even flip which happens first. Relativity permits this only for events too far apart for light to connect (spacelike pairs), so no message, no cause, no effect ever runs backwards. Simultaneity is a convention of your motion; causality is not.

Physics: Lorentz transform with c = 1; the teal grid is the set of lines t′ = const and x′ = const drawn in the lab's coordinates, so its shear IS the transform. Calibration hyperbolae t²−x² = ±1 mark one unit of proper time and proper length on every axis, however tilted. Check: the interval s² = x²−t² of each event reads the same in both frames.
