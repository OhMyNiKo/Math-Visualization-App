Inverse Functions and the Mirror y = x

Choose a function, then drag the fold slider (or press Replay) to watch every point of f swing over the line y = x and land on the graph of f⁻¹. Drag the green point along f: its mirror image (a, b) → (b, a), both derivatives and the swapped domain and range recompute live. Type your own f and the module checks whether it is one-to-one before inverting it.

The defining equations \(\displaystyle f^{-1}(f(x)) = x \) \(\displaystyle \big(f^{-1}\big)'(b) = \dfrac{1}{f'(a)} \) where \(\displaystyle b = f(a) \)

The misconception this kills

"f⁻¹ means 1/f" is false. The −1 is a functional inverse, the undo button, not a reciprocal: f⁻¹(f(a)) gives a back, while 1/f(a) is just another number. Right now ?.

Mathematics: the fold is the true 3-D rotation of the plane about the line y = x, projected back (each point keeps its component along the mirror and its perpendicular component scales by cos πt); preset inverses and derivatives are analytic, a custom f is inverted by 60 steps of bisection on its longest monotone run and differentiated by a central difference.
