Higher Derivatives and Concavity

Pick a function or type your own, and see \( f \), \( f' \) and \( f'' \) drawn together. The background is tinted where the curve bends upward (\( f''>0 \)) and where it bends downward (\( f''<0 \)); the violet dots are the inflection points, where the bending switches. Drag the probe \( x^\ast \) along the curve to watch the tangent line and the osculating circle flip sides.

The defining test \(\displaystyle f''(x)>0 \Rightarrow \) concave up, \(\displaystyle f''(x)<0 \Rightarrow \) concave down · inflection where \(\displaystyle f'' \) changes sign · radius of curvature \(\displaystyle \rho = \dfrac{(1+f'^2)^{3/2}}{|f''|} \)

The misconception this kills

"\( f''(c)=0 \) means an inflection point at \( c \)." It does not. Zero second derivative is necessary for a smooth inflection, never sufficient: the sign of \( f'' \) must actually change.
