Dot Product, Cross Product

Two vectors, two very different products. Drag the tips of \( \mathbf a \) and \( \mathbf b \) in the plane on the left: the amber shadow is the projection of \( \mathbf a \) onto \( \mathbf b \) and the arc colour is the sign of \( \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b \). On the right the same two vectors sit in 3-D with their cross product standing on the parallelogram they span; drag there to turn the view, and use the z sliders to lift the vectors out of the plane.

The defining equations \(\displaystyle \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta,\quad \mathbf a\times\mathbf b = |\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\theta\,\hat{\mathbf n} \) · a number and a vector: \(\displaystyle \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b = a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_z \), \(\displaystyle \mathbf a\times\mathbf b=(a_yb_z-a_zb_y,\ a_zb_x-a_xb_z,\ a_xb_y-a_yb_x) \)

Controls

Drag the arrowheads in the left panel to move a and b; the sliders do the same thing with numbers. z-components only show in the 3-D panel.

The misconception this kills

"The dot product and the cross product are two ways of multiplying vectors to get another vector." No. \( \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b \) is a number (a scalar, no direction); \( \mathbf a\times\mathbf b \) is a vector perpendicular to both. And \( \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=0 \) does not make either vector zero: it makes them perpendicular. Drag the arc to blue and check: right now ?.

Mathematics: dot product by components, angle by \( \theta=\operatorname{atan2}(|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|,\ \mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b) \) (well-conditioned near 0° and 180°), projection \( (\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b)/|\mathbf b| \), full 3-D cross product by the determinant formula; the 3-D panel is an orthographic projection with yaw ψ and 28° elevation.
